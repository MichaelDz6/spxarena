# Investment risk guide — source notes

Reviewed October 19, 2026. Page: `/investment-risk/`, English and Polish.

- SEC Investor.gov, “Gauge Your Risk Tolerance”: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/save-and-invest/gauge-your-risk-tolerance
  - Investor education guidance against risky investments for short-term goals of five years or less. This is guidance, not a statistical loss-probability estimate.
- Zhu, Tan, Ahluwalia and Dutton, Vanguard research, October 2023, “Constructing return-target portfolios”: https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/constructing_return_target_portfolios.pdf
  - Figure 1 / printed page 3: rolling ten-year annualized nominal US equity returns approximately −5% to +20%; observations 1926–2023, S&P Composite before 1957 and S&P 500 thereafter. Source data FactSet and Robert Shiller. Overlapping windows are not independent trials. The page does not calculate loss probabilities or imply an ETF existed throughout this sample.
  - Companion article (November 27, 2023): https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/articles/improving-odds-meeting-portfolio-return-target.html explicitly dates the return sample through June 2023.
- Pástor and Stambaugh (2012), “Are Stocks Really Less Volatile in the Long Run?”, The Journal of Finance 67(2), 431–478: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2012.01722.x
  - Published abstract and conclusion: predictive systems using up to 206 years of real equity returns; parameter uncertainty and imperfect predictors can outweigh mean reversion. Model-based predictive variance, not a universal probability of loss or optimal duration. Do not conflate its real-return model with Vanguard’s nominal historical sample.
- SEC Investor.gov, asset allocation: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation
- SEC Investor.gov, ETF bulletin: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-24
- SEC Investor.gov, financial foundations: https://www.investor.gov/build-wealth-over-time-through-saving-and-investing

The three horizon cards are an editorial planning framework, explicitly labelled as such. Five years comes from SEC guidance; ten years illustrates that even a decade has historical losses, not a scientific safety threshold. No optimal duration or guaranteed recovery date is asserted. Currency, inflation, fees, tax and withdrawal timing can change an individual's result.
